The direct national SII download makes it possible to inspect construction lines within each property record. A record whose primary use is commercial or another code may contain a construction line with use H. Filtering only on primary use H can therefore leave out residential use already recorded within non-H records. The lines capture the presence of use H, but not how many census dwellings it represents: this cross-check counts administrative records and does not establish omitted dwellings.

Across the non-agricultural series, 90,680 non-H records contain at least one valid H line. After excluding 14,184 common-property records and 3 referenced parent records, 76,493 distinct records remain. Because they do not overlap the 6,057,949 primary-H records in the same file, their union expands the non-agricultural count with residential evidence to 6,134,442 and reduces the national arithmetic difference with the Census by 76,493. This does not turn each record into a dwelling and is not the sum of positive commune gaps.

The direct download contains 6,057,949 records with primary use H: 3,141 more than the July mirror's 6,054,808 used in the original analysis, and the same aggregate as the cited MINVU control. Aggregate agreement is not a complete record-level reconciliation, so it does not replace the original figures, bars or scenarios.

In this new cut, the sum of positive commune differences falls from 1,588,389 to 1,514,765: a reduction of 73,624, smaller than the national net reduction because some communes already had non-positive balances and others cross zero. Relative to the July mirror, the total increase in the record count is 79,634: 3,141 from source and coverage plus 76,493 from expanding the filter; it is not a pure filter effect.

The agricultural series remains separate: it records 941 roles with an H line and 19,462 with a P construction—a farmhouse—but no H. These are property records, not dwellings, and are not added to the non-agricultural group.

SII. A valid H line requires a positive ordinal and area. Multiple lines in one record count once. A K record or a record referenced as common property is excluded first, followed by a referenced parent record; the individual condominium member remains. Unknown or empty uses are not imputed. Primary-H records without a valid H line also remain classified as H: the missing line does not establish absence of residential use.

CASEN. Quantities are aggregated from one household response, taken from its head's record when v9=3/4; residents are the people belonging to those households. They are not filtered by main-household status (v28), which is asked only in dwellings with more than one household. National and regional estimates use expr. The two commune cases use expc for descriptive purposes only.

What can be combined. Within the same SII download, primary-H records and distinct non-H records with a valid H line can be joined. The agricultural series also counts property records, but it is reported separately because it lies outside the chosen non-agricultural universe. CASEN and informal-settlement households are different units and their overlap is uncontrolled; they are not added to that union. The original 2024H2 sensitivity and fiscal scenarios remain intact.
