Causal ML heterogeneity (BLP, GATE and CLAN)
−12
in every hundred in the most fragile quintile (G1)

In the quintile with the largest predicted harm (G1: 99.5% in secondary school, 77.2% vocational-technical, 87.1% prior attendance), about 12 fewer in every hundred enter higher education when only their individual backpack is controlled for; once the school's prior history is added, that drop is absorbed (−0.26 pp).

GATE in G1 with student controls: −12.26 pp in overall entry (−17.79 to −6.74; BLP +0.634, p = 0.0243), −11.48 pp in persistence and −8.02 pp in IP/CFT (p = 0.0007). With G−5 school history, G1 overall entry moves to −0.26 pp, yet IP/CFT heterogeneity persists (BLP +0.425, p = 0.0177).

Fine print and sensitivity checks

What survives the stress tests and what remains open

Before drawing policy lessons, I subjected the findings to four boundary and sensitivity checks:

  • Part III debt settled: grade lag (+5.29 pp) withstands 2.54 times the prior trend bound (0.81 pp) and holds at +3.87 pp among G−2 stayers
  • Cinelli and Hazlett (2020): erasing the −2.09 pp IP/CFT drop requires a hidden confounder over twice as strong as G−5 history (Robustness Value 6.16%; 2.33% at 95%)
  • Substitution vs. net loss: university entry is +1.98 pp (−0.22 to +4.19); part of the −2.09 pp IP/CFT drop may be diversion, and the individual-trajectory test is still pending
  • PAES/PDT (2021–2026): with equal history, test-taking (−10.98 pp), enrolling (−7.46) and top 20% (−2.54) cease to differ from zero (+0.75, +2.87 and +1.75 pp)

So what now for public policy?

When a high school closes, relocation does not end with a seat in March

The recipe depends on the grade level at closure; these are precautionary safeguards, not estimated effects:

  • Upper elementary (Grades 7 to 8): nearby seats, transport and pedagogical welcome to cushion the immediate grade lag
  • Secondary school (Grades 9 to 11): named early-warning support for vocational-technical students with low prior attendance (G1 profile)
  • Vocational-technical articulation: specialty credit recognition and direct guidance into accredited IPs and CFTs
  • Financial bridge: timely FUAS completion (tuition-free aid and scholarships) and follow-up across the first two postsecondary years
