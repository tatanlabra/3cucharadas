A map shows buildings, not the children who have to move.

When a school closes, it often seems enough that another school is nearby. But that says nothing about whether its students kept studying or moved up a grade the next year.

What I did (2005–2024)

I followed every student, year by year, across twenty cohorts

78,972students who lost their school between 2005 and 2024
1,141closed regular schools with matched controls (out of 1,648 clean RCID v1 closures)
734,472similar students from schools that stayed open, for comparison

I reconstructed 79,709,054 enrolment records and 73,873,835 performance records (2004–2025), matching by year, sex, age, region, provider type, rurality, level and grade.

The first thing that shows up

Across all 19 measurable cohorts, the gap points the same way

Each bar is one annual cohort: how much more the share of students who fell behind a grade or had no enrolment the next year rose, per hundred students, relative to their peers.

Annual range from +0.94 to +28.22 percentage points (median +7.18). Several years have few closures and wide uncertainty; that is why the full series matters.

Weighting by exposed students (Callaway and Sant'Anna), about 9 more in every hundred fell behind or left the register the next year, relative to their peers (+6 under random effects). This is an adjusted gap, not a confirmed causal effect.

Callaway and Sant'Anna: +9.11 percentage points (+5.34 to +12.88; Manski bounds +0.43 to +16.21) and +1.38 in two-year non-enrolment. Random effects: +6.29 (+4.49 to +8.09; prediction +0.09 to +12.49).

Fine print and stress tests (2016–2019)

I still cannot say the closure is the cause on its own

Grade lag rises +3.87 points among stayers from two years prior (+9.58 among late entrants) and withstands 2.54 times the prior trend bound (0.81 points). Yet those schools were already more fragile, and nearly half of their students were already switching schools before closure:

Exposed students with at least one prior school switch (G−3 to G−1)
47.04%
Comparison group with at least one prior switch
30.52%

With at least 90% valid records the gap is +4.26 points (bounds +0.09 to +7.84). Moving to a receiving school with higher prior promotion cushions −3.35 points (+2.03 versus +5.38), a descriptive difference without its own interval.

So what now?

A result with causal limits already supports minimum safeguards

These are not estimated effects: they are the minimum standard of care before reorganising a school network:

  • Neither proximity nor an informative relocation notice counts as continuity
  • Real vacancies and safe transport verified before deciding
  • Support and funding for the school that receives the students
  • Guidance for priority students and families through the change
  • Tracking every student by name for at least two years
