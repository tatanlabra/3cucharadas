TITLE: JEV-style local judges in three spoonfuls: when voting improves a decision and when it only stalls it
URL: https://3cucharadas.cl/en/ia-herramientas/datos-codigo/jueces-locales-cpu-laya-vega-arc/

AUTHOR COMMENT:
Hi HN,

Inspired by the JEV Ultrafast demo (which separates fast System 1 action selection from LLM text generation to run browser tasks in 7.1 s), I wanted to see if I could build a 100% local, open-weights equivalent (`simil-jev`) on a work laptop CPU (`Intel Core i7-8550U`, 15 W TDP, 23.19 GiB RAM, no discrete GPU) to filter verification gates (`"Yes"`, `"No"`, `"Undefined"`) without calling an LLM.

Because no sub-billion-parameter classifier is reliable enough on its own, I combined three open models with very different architectures under a 2-of-3 majority quorum with abstention across 240 cases (80 calibration, 160 out-of-sample test cases: 60 `"Yes"`, 60 `"No"`, 40 `"Undefined"`):
- **Laya 0.4.11** (non-autoregressive System 1 engine trained with strictly proper scoring rules): 2.26 GiB RAM, 13.83 s cold, 366 ms p95.
- **VegaML 0.11.0** (`14.3M` potential-well physics engine on top of a frozen `Qwen3.5-0.8B` backbone): 4.93 GiB RAM, 13.66 s cold, 1,717 ms p95.
- **Arc Decide 0.4B** (ONNX Runtime INT8 classifier): 0.59 GiB RAM, 4.21 s cold, 950 ms p95.

What happened in the benchmark:
1. **Why `"No"` vanished (`0/60` recall):** Laya behaved like an optimist (`125/160` `"Yes"` votes, `73` false positives, only `1` `"No"` vote on negatives). VegaML behaved like a pessimist (`131/160` `"No"` votes, `0/60` on `"Yes"`). Arc Decide stayed silent under its `0.66` threshold on `148/160` cases (`12` `"Yes"` votes, `0` `"No"` votes). Because a 2-of-3 rejection needs two `"No"` ballots, not a single negative case out of 60 was ever rejected.
2. **The `66.67%` selective accuracy trap:** Laya and VegaML voted opposite on `101/160` cases while Arc abstained. The panel only emitted a verdict on `15/160` cases (`9.38%` coverage), getting `10/15` right (`66.67%`, Wilson 95% CI `41.7%` to `84.8%`, after overfitting `14/14` on calibration). On overall 3-class accuracy it scored `28.75%` (`46/160`, where `36` hits were just abstentions landing on the `40` `"Undefined"` cases)—barely above a `0 ms` script that always returns `"Undefined"` (`25.00%`), while taking `~31.7 s` cold in serial CPU subprocesses.
3. **What I want to test next (not measured yet):** Gate verification against a rule is formally 3-class Natural Language Inference (NLI: `entailment`, `contradiction`, `neutral`). Instead of the trio (~7.8 GiB of RAM combined), my next experiment is a single bidirectional NLI cross-encoder, `cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small` (DeBERTaV3, `44M` backbone parameters, fine-tuned on SNLI + MultiNLI, with an ONNX INT8 build for AVX2 CPUs in its repository). I have not run it on this bank, so its latency, RAM use, and whether it can actually say `"No"` on these gates are open questions, not results.

Happy to discuss the calibration failure modes, Condorcet's jury theorem assumptions (`p > 0.5` and error independence), or how you would set up a fair NLI comparison on CPU.
